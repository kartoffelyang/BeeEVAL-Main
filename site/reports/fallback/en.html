SPECIAL REPORT / OCTOBER 2026

When Cockpit AI Cannot Help
From Failure Responses to Task Recovery

An analysis of AI failure-handling mechanisms based on actual BeeEVAL test records: where the system supports the request, where it returns the burden to the user, and what enterprises should improve first.

01 / EXECUTIVE JUDGMENT

Enterprises should treat the ability to continue fulfilling a need after failure as a product capability

The sample already includes phrasing guidance, manual instructions, UI handoffs, similar-feature alternatives, network retries and capability-boundary explanations. Yet the same fallback method can either help users continue or leave them repeating themselves, following incorrect entry points or drifting away from the goal.

Executive recommendation: fix loops, misleading feedback and incorrect handoffs before expanding open-ended Q&A coverage.

Prioritize genuine recovery paths for frequent needs: correct entry points, specific controls, retained constraints and service continuity. Stronger models can add capabilities, but do not automatically complete UI and service workflows.

Separate failure types to assign responsibility

Misunderstanding, missing knowledge, unavailable integration, temporary faults, insufficient conditions and prohibited actions require different owners and handling paths. A uniform “I am still learning” response hides the actual problem.

Make a safe stop a good experience too

When conditions or permissions are missing, explain boundaries and provide permitted alternatives. Intent understanding and execution authorization must be assessed separately; appropriate refusals should not be forced into automatic completion.

Do not equate opening a page with completion

Whether the page is correct, parameters are retained, users can find the action and the task ultimately succeeds all require subsequent evidence. “Opened” in a response only proves that the system said so.

Avoid optimizing only first-attempt success

Report first-attempt automatic completion, post-failure recovery, appropriate refusal and unknown outcomes separately. Otherwise, effective manual handoffs may be undervalued and seemingly proactive outcomes that lose constraints may be overestimated.

These are sample-based product and organizational recommendations, not estimates of current market adoption, vehicle capability rankings or commercial returns.

04 / BEEEVAL FINDINGS

Eight findings point to the same issue: recovery paths need design and verification

Finding 01

Specific guidance reduces user effort; “try manually” offers limited help

NIO ET5 responds to a passenger-seat request with “I do not support this feature yet; try operating it manually.” Li Auto L9 Livis identifies a mirror switch on the driver's door; AITO M9 identifies the washer button for windshield cleaning. These three records show different levels of specificity, not overall vehicle superiority.[B01][B15][B28]

Enterprise implication:Create retested mappings of this vehicle's controls, accurate locations and permitted states for key features, reducing search effort between voice failure and manual operation.

Finding 02

Opening a UI is a valuable handoff, but the next turn also needs support

ZEEKR 9X claims to open ambient-lighting settings. Xiaomi Yu7's HUD record describes an opened and highlighted page but no further guidance after a height follow-up. A page transition can reduce steps but does not prove completion.[B10][B25]

Enterprise implication:Connect dialogue to UI state: retain the goal, focus and parameters, and allow the user to ask what to do next. For touchscreen workflows unsuitable while driving, use appropriate physical controls or defer the task.

Finding 03

Repetition after a user correction is a clear break in the fallback process

The NIO ET5 user follows the suggested seat-to-default phrasing, but the system repeats the example. The Xiaomi Yu7 user says AC is already on, but the system repeats the same checklist. AITO M9 repeatedly returns control-command examples to a question about snow driving mode.[B03][B24][B29]

Enterprise implication:Remember eliminated causes and count failures for the same goal. Escalating by default after a second similar failure is a proposed starting rule that must be adjusted for risk and user research.

Finding 04

Alternatives must retain constraints and authorization

ZEEKR 9X says it has no air purifier and claims to enable intelligent deodorization. NIO ET5 suggests other resources for a charging-station request requiring nighttime opening. The records prove neither feature equivalence nor nighttime availability of the alternative station.[B09][B06]

Enterprise implication:Show conditions as satisfied, unsatisfied or unknown. Explain differences and request confirmation when alternatives change the goal; verify actual state after execution. A similar feature must not be presented as success on the original task.

Finding 05

Location lists cannot automatically replace comparison, rules or prediction answers

NIO ET5 presents a choice for cheaper parking and also converts a question about temporary parking permission into parking-lot selection. Li Auto L9 Livis describes clickable prices, but comparison remains the user's job.[B07][B08][B20]

Enterprise implication:Retain the relationship the user wants: comparison, filtering, explanation or prediction. Expose missing prices, spaces or opening hours instead of masking them with a related list.

Finding 06

Boundary explanations must be accurate; instructions must fit this vehicle

ZEEKR 9X explains that wheel-alignment parameters are not integrated and lists supported states. Xiaomi Yu7 explicitly says it has not confirmed one-pedal support on this vehicle but gives a generic path. AITO M9 gives a specific sound-simulation path that this report has not independently verified.[B14][B26][B30]

Enterprise implication:Bind knowledge and feature explanations to vehicle, configuration and software version. Without adaptation evidence, provide verifiable vehicle-specific references; do not generate seemingly precise paths from general knowledge.

Finding 07

Fault reporting needs a service workflow

The Li Auto L9 Livis user wants to report reduced cooling; the reply only says AC is on. Xiaomi Yu7's cooling checklist mentions a service center, but information the user has already checked does not advance the process. Route service intent separately from switch control and general Q&A.[B19][B24]

Enterprise implication:Carry symptoms, completed checks, vehicle information and consented necessary data into the service entry point. A handoff is complete only when a ticket or request actually exists and follow-up responsibility is assigned.

Finding 08

Recovery clues exist, but do not justify a recovery-rate claim

Xiaomi Yu7's automatic-high-beam record describes a tester manually enabling the feature after a voice response of unsupported. This is closer to recovery evidence than failure phrasing alone, but the original video has not been checked and user-effort data is absent.[B22]

Enterprise implication:Continuously collect subsequent paths and results for initially failed tasks. Record human intervention, clicks, elapsed time and retained constraints before calculating recovery rates.

06 / ENTERPRISE DECISIONS

Establish genuine capabilities and follow-up paths before refining wording

The cockpit product owner should coordinate fallback as part of version delivery. Model, interaction, vehicle-control, ecosystem and aftersales teams should each own their verifiable part. Prioritize recovery for existing frequent needs, interfaces and genuine entry points; determine additional model investment from the remaining problems.

Priority / Enterprise decisionOwnerVerifiable deliverableCompletion criterion
P0 Correct loops and false completion feedbackProduct owner + Model / Quality teamsRepeated-failure detection, memory of eliminated causes and actual-state acknowledgments; high-risk retest listNo repeated ineffective prompts in the specified regression set; no completion claim without state evidence; safety boundaries apply correctly
P0 Maintain a version-specific capability tableVehicle control / Platform ownerMapping of vehicle, configuration, feature, permitted state, voice availability, controls and page entry pointsEvery released fallback instruction works on the matching vehicle version; needs with no entry point are explicitly marked
P1 Retain the goal in UI handoffsInteraction + Application ownersReliable page transitions, highlighting, parameter transfer, continuous follow-up and return mechanismsGoal and constraints survive transfer; no repeated description is needed; the page is correct and operable
P1 Transparent constraints and confirmed alternativesTravel / Ecosystem ownerCondition states for price, time and availability; explanation and confirmation of alternative differencesMark the original goal as incomplete when appropriate; do not relax key conditions without consent or treat unknown data as satisfied
P1 Integrate fault reports with serviceAftersales digitalization + Service ownerService entry point, necessary context, user authorization, ticket or request acknowledgment and receiving teamA genuine reference number or acceptance state; explicit responsibilities and service commitments; completed checks visible to the human team
P2 Strengthen models, search or offline capabilities according to causeAI platform ownerSourced vehicle-specific knowledge, tool-error handling and a tested offline feature listBetter recovery on the same task set; no increase in incorrect execution or guidance; acceptable latency and service costs

Proposed single-task handling workflow

01 / ClassifyIdentify why it failed

Read actual service, vehicle and permission states; model uncertainty is only one signal.

02 / RepairObtain the minimum necessary information

Ask targeted questions about targets, parameters or authorization; remember confirmed and eliminated information.

03 / ContinueChoose an available recovery entry point

Select vehicle-specific controls, the correct page, trusted retrieval or a service entry point according to scenario and driving state.

04 / RetainCarry the goal and constraints

Retain target, degree, time, negative conditions and completed steps; explain differences for alternatives.

05 / VerifyReport state using actual results

Distinguish not executed, partially completed, completed, transferred and unconfirmed; avoid verbal claims of success without evidence.

06 / EscalateEnd the loop at the boundary

After a limited number of attempts, change the path or stop explicitly; give asynchronous tasks an acknowledgment and follow-up query entry point.

Two proposed minimum rules:Do not claim success when it cannot be confirmed; do not silently execute an alternative that changes the key goal. Judge safety and authorization boundaries independently, without crossing them to pursue recovery rates.

How to assess whether investment is worthwhile

Establish a baseline on a specific task set, accounting for extra user time, repeated actions, service-transfer costs, model-call costs and repeated aftersales diagnostics. Expand coverage only when final completion improves under the same constraints, user cost falls and service cost remains acceptable. This sample has no traffic, support-cost or retention data, so it does not estimate ROI or claim increased sales or renewals.

Expansion and exit conditions:If an entry point is not genuine, the receiving service cannot fulfill its commitment, key constraints are lost or side effects cannot be controlled, stop that automatic handoff route and retain a transparent human entry point. If stronger models do not improve recovery, prioritize integration and workflow repairs over adding model calls.

07 / ACCEPTANCE & EVALUATION

Report first-attempt success, failure recovery and appropriate refusal separately

Records labeled as first-attempt automatic failures can still contain useful guidance or manual recovery. Preserve two testing stages: assess the original task, then the post-failure path and actual result. The same failure label must not be used to directly infer that fallback is ineffective.

Proposed metricDefinition / DenominatorHow to obtain evidence
First-attempt automatic completion rateCorrect first-attempt automatic completions ÷ applicable tasks with known outcomes; appropriate refusals and inapplicable tasks reported separatelyStandardize task, initial state and goal constraints; check actual execution results
Post-failure recovery rateTasks meeting the original goal or a confirmed alternative within a preset time / turn limit after initial failure ÷ applicable initially failed tasks with observable subsequent outcomesContinuously record subsequent actions and outcomes; report automatic, user-manual and human-service completion separately
Subsequent-outcome observability rateApplicable initially failed tasks with known subsequent outcomes ÷ all applicable initially failed tasksList unknown outcomes separately to avoid bias from tracking only easily recoverable tasks
Entry-point validity rateInstructions leading to an actually reachable entry point that supports the intended action ÷ tested entry-point instructionsFollow each path on the matching vehicle, configuration and software version; written paths alone are insufficient
Context and constraint retention rateHandoff tasks retaining all necessary goals and constraints ÷ tested handoff tasksCheck target, time, degree, negative conditions, completed checks and required authorization
Repetition-loop rateTasks still repeating ineffective responses after a preset threshold ÷ applicable initially failed tasks with observable outcomesTrack the same goal; a second similar failure is the proposed escalation trigger, with an adjustable threshold
Additional user costAdditional turns, clicks, time and repeated descriptions required after failure; report distributions separatelyRecord user operations and timing, with actual user testing; do not estimate from AI comments
Incorrect guidance / False completion / Unauthorized substitutionReport counts, severity and testing opportunities separately; do not conceal them with an average experience scoreCompare with actual state, original video, applicable manuals and user authorization
Quality of appropriate refusalProportion of refusal-required scenarios with correct boundaries, accurate explanations and actionable permitted alternativesUse a separate refusal test set; do not mix it into the success-rate denominator for actions that must be executed

Six scenario groups requiring additional testing

① Identical repetition and rephrasing; ② continued failure after following the system's prompt; ③ explicit user corrections or eliminated steps; ④ offline conditions, timeouts and non-execution by devices; ⑤ alternatives violating price, time or feature constraints; ⑥ nonexistent pages, insufficient permissions or unavailable humans. For physical actions, observe vehicle state and side effects too.

Proposed acceptance principles:False completion reports for key controls, unconfirmed changes to key goals and incorrectly adapted instructions should have independent release gates based on severity. Zero observed events does not prove zero risk; report test coverage and scenario counts together.

Numerical targets for general recovery, elapsed time and user burden should be set after the initial baseline and user research. This report does not prescribe unsupported industry percentages.

All of the above are proposed additions to evaluation design, not claims that current BeeEVAL tools already provide these statistics or scales. AI analysis can assist screening, but final recovery and safety judgments need corresponding tests and human verification.

08 / 90-DAY PLAN

Validate value through a clearly bounded recovery project

The following is a proposed implementation cadence, not a committed delivery schedule. Enterprises should adapt it to existing systems, feature interfaces and service resources.

Days 1–30

Retesting and baseline

Reproduce selected scenarios on the current version; review original video and vehicle configuration. Select 20–30 frequent or high-impact tasks, identify six failure causes and genuine entry points, and establish baselines for initial outcomes and subsequent recovery.

Deliverables:Verified case set, capability table, owners and metric definitions. Preserve historical notes for old issues that cannot be reproduced.

Days 31–60

Fix P0 issues and connect a few paths

Eliminate repeated prompts and success wording without acknowledgments. First connect 5–10 genuine UI or control guidance paths, then at least one fault-service entry point with contextual handoff.

Deliverables:Demonstrable end-to-end workflow, adaptation verification, constraint and authorization checks, and regression records.

Days 61–90

User validation and expansion decisions

Compare before and after on the same task set; add actual users and suitable driving contexts. Retest offline conditions, timeouts, wrong entry points and unavailable humans; calculate cost per recovery.

Deliverables:Recovery and user-cost report, issue list and expand / repair / stop decisions.

Final executive decision criteria:Did users fulfill their needs with less effort? Were key constraints and authorization retained? Can the system accurately report state? Can the service team assume follow-up responsibility?
